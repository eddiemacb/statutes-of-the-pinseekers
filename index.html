Ratified by the Foursome · Enforceable at the Bar

Statutes of the Pinseekers

Being the Charter, Code of Conduct, and Schedule of Fines governing the Weekly Round, drafted in the spirit of fairness and enforced in the spirit of spite.

WHEREAS the undersigned Members have played golf together weekly for longer than any of them can accurately recall, and have accumulated grievances in proportion;

WHEREAS disputes over etiquette, curses, mulligans, and unpaid skins have historically been settled by shouting on the 9th tee, to the detriment of pace of play and civility;

WHEREAS a golf ball does not know it has been complimented, and yet somehow always seems to know;

the Members do hereby ordain and establish this Charter, to be interpreted liberally in favor of whoever is currently losing.

Article I

Definitions

The Jinx
Any verbal compliment, encouragement, or prediction of success offered before a shot's outcome is final. See §1.
The Pot
The communal fund into which all fines are paid, disbursed at season's end toward drinks, dinner, or a trophy of deliberately poor taste.
The Starter
Any Member designated to witness this Charter's signing. Holds no enforcement power, but is listened to anyway.
Commissioner
The Member who posted the worst score in the preceding round. Serves a one-week term breaking tied rulings, as penance.
Leather
The traditional maximum distance a putt may be conceded as a "gimme" — the length of a grip laid flat, no longer.

Article II

Rules of Conduct & Etiquette

  1. The Curse of the Compliment No Member shall say "nice putt," "you're good," "that's in," or any equivalent phrase before a ball has come to rest in the hole. The putter is entitled, and encouraged, to blame any resulting miss entirely on the speaker. Fine — see Art. III §1
  2. Premature Fairway Call Declaring a tee shot "in the fairway" or "safe" before it has landed and stopped moving invites identical misfortune and identical blame. Fine — see Art. III §2
  3. Breach of the Silent Backswing No talking, cart engines, club rattling, or ringtones during another Member's takeaway. The window of silence begins at the waggle and ends at contact. Fine — see Art. III §3
  4. The Undeclared Mulligan A second ball may be played from the tee only if the word "mulligan" is spoken aloud before the club is taken back. A silently re-teed ball counts, no exceptions, no appeals. Fine — see Art. III §4
  5. Blaming the Equipment Attributing a poor shot to one's clubs, grips, balls, shoes, or "the wind that only I felt" is permitted but not free of charge. Fine — see Art. III §5
  6. The Extended Brag A Member may celebrate a genuinely good shot for the remainder of that hole and the one following. Any mention beyond the second subsequent tee box is taxed per additional hole. Fine — see Art. III §6
  7. Unsolicited Swing Advice No Member below a demonstrated handicap advantage of at least five strokes over the listener may offer swing tips unasked. Advice must be requested in clear, unambiguous language. Fine — see Art. III §7
  8. Failure to Rake or Repair Bunkers shall be raked and pitch marks repaired before the offending Member leaves the green or bunker. Discovery by a later group is a strict-liability offense. Fine — see Art. III §8
  9. The Visible or Audible Phone Phones must be silenced for the round. A phone that rings, buzzes audibly, or is visibly consulted during another Member's swing is a violation regardless of intent. Fine — see Art. III §9
  10. Failure to Be Ready A Member whose turn is approaching must be club-in-hand and thinking about the shot, not the previous hole's argument, by the time play reaches them. Fine — see Art. III §10
  11. The Generous Self-Gimme No Member may concede their own putt as "good" beyond one leather's length without a second Member's independent confirmation. Fine — see Art. III §11
  12. Late Settlement of Wagers All skins, presses, and side bets are due and payable before the group leaves the 19th hole. Debts carried home accrue interest at the Commissioner's discretion. Fine — see Art. III §12

Article IV

Enforcement & Adjudication

Any Member may call a violation upon its occurrence. A call made more than one hole after the offense is void for staleness.

Disputed calls are settled by majority vote of Members present. A tied vote is broken by the Commissioner, whose ruling may not be appealed, questioned, or brought up again on the drive home.

Fines are payable on the spot, in cash, or by written IOU redeemable no later than the following round's first tee.

Article V

Amendments

This Charter may be amended by unanimous consent of all Members present, given no earlier than the 19th hole and no later than the second round of drinks.

IN WITNESS WHEREOF, the undersigned Members, being of reasonably sound mind for a Saturday morning, do hereby subscribe to this Charter and submit themselves to its fines, in full knowledge of what they've agreed to.
